# Both-halves cyclicity: an additional proved small-order exclusion

**Claim (proved).** In every successful minimum-length `2n−4` call gossip schedule on `4≤n≤10` vertices, at least one of the two chronological halves has an acyclic contact graph.

**Proof.** For `n≤7`, the v1.1 sharp half-forest theorem proves that *both* halves are forests. Suppose therefore `8≤n≤10` and that both halves contain a cycle. Let `r,s` be their respective numbers of connected components. Each half has `n−2` distinct edges; if it contains a cycle, its components number at least `n−(n−2)+1=3`. Thus `r,s≥3`. By the universal prefix/suffix intersection lemma, every prefix component intersects every suffix component, so `n≥rs≥9`. Hence `n∈{9,10}`, and as `3×4>10`, necessarily `r=s=3`.

Each of the three suffix components meets all three prefix components, so it has at least three vertices. Since `n≤10`, at least two suffix components must have **exactly three** vertices. Each of these three-vertex components has one vertex from each of the three distinct prefix components. Their three original secrets are therefore mutually unknown across those vertices immediately after the prefix. For all three to know all three secrets by the end, these three vertices need at least **three** internal calls (two different contacts cannot accomplish three-agent gossip). So the two size-three suffix components each consume ≥3 calls. The third suffix component has at least three vertices and must be connected, so it consumes ≥2 calls; for `n=10` it has four vertices and must consume ≥3 calls. Therefore at least `3+3+2=8` suffix calls are needed at `n=9` (but only `n−2=7` exist), or `3+3+3=9` at `n=10` (but only `8` exist). Contradiction. The cases `n≤8` follow already from the earlier theorem and the product `rs≥9>n`. QED.

**Boundary:** Does every `2n−4` gossip schedule have at least one forest half, for arbitrary `n`? This remains **OPEN**. A dedicated stochastic counterexample search (`both_halves_search.cpp`) failed to find schedules with both halves cyclic in 1,200,000 mutation proposals each for `n=11,12,13,14`. This is **heuristic**, biased, and not a proof for those sizes.

**Novelty:** This small-order corollary may overlap minimum-gossip kernel results (Labahn 1995) and call-reordering theory (Krumme 1996). It is not claimed to be publication-level novelty. The core `2n−3` conjecture and POD remain open.
