## The precise priority decision

**Already known and not independently novel:** 2n-4 lower bound, standard two-contact new-vertex extension (Göbel–Cerdeira–Veldman 1991, Theorem 13), static Cd-graph forest decompositions, two-ended binary-forest information-flow, complete admissible p-grid permutation criterion, isomorphism classification, linear-extension/reordering machinery.

**Not explicitly located in either supplied complete paper:**
1. The **original-order first-bridge covering** implication: when a suffix tree begins with its forced bridge between distinct singleton prefix intersections, every third prefix component's secrets must already be carried on both sides of the bridge. The exact intersection cardinality 1/2/3 deductions are not stated in these papers under these hypotheses.
2. The exact finite proposition: **every** successful 22-contact distinct-edge two-way gossip schedule on 13 vertices has a forest in at least one **unchanged chronological half**, i.e. its first or last eleven contacts form a forest.

**Important difference between absence of statement and mathematical independence:** We have not proved that neither result can be derived as a corollary of Labahn's complete classification, Krumme's underlying structural lemmas, or other older publications. In fact, a complete kernel classification *could* support such a finite derived theorem with additional case analysis. The first-bridge argument may be regarded as an elementary information-conservation consequence rather than a significant standalone discovery. **Novelty status: POSSIBLY DISTINCT EXPLICIT CLAIM, NOT HISTORICALLY CERTIFIED NEW.**

### Quantifier argument, now tied to original theorem statements

From Krumme Theorem 1 and Corollary 7 we obtain, roughly, a property of **some rearrangement** of each complete schedule. The n13 theorem concerns **the original given sequence**. Existential reordering does not entail fixed-half membership in every permissible chronology. Our previously independently certified n8 and n13 same-event-poset pairs have different chronological midpoint cycle ranks. That establishes a real difference in the *half-cycle statistic*, but does not show that the weaker at-least-one-half-forest statement cannot follow from a strengthened kernel theorem.

Similarly Labahn Theorem 5.2 and Krumme Corollary 3 are graph-edge **existential partitions**. Our v1.6 witness has a static qualifying partition into two forests while an actual original half is cyclic; therefore a classical static decomposition does **not by itself** fix original half cycle ranks.
